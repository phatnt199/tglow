v0.10.0 — a caret you can move

Reported plainly: you could not move the cursor to fix a letter. The composer
had no caret at all. Typing appended to the end and backspace took from the
end, so correcting a typo in the middle of a message meant deleting
everything after it and typing that again.

It has one now. `←` and `→` move it, `<C-a>` and `<C-e>` go to the ends,
`<C-w>` takes the word behind it, and `Delete` takes the character under it.
Backspace removes what is before the caret rather than whatever happens to be
last.

The caret counts whole characters, not bytes and not code points. Vietnamese
is why, and it is not hypothetical: a terminal delivers `ế` as three separate
key presses, two of them a bare mark that joins the letter before it. Anything
counting smaller than a whole character drifts a position on each one, and the
next mark lands on the wrong letter — `abcd` becoming `abêćd`. One press
crosses `ế`; one backspace removes it.

The caret is drawn as a reversed cell rather than a block laid over the text,
so the letter under it stays readable, and the draft scrolls to follow the
caret instead of always showing the end. A draft with newlines in it — from
`<A-Enter>`, or from `e` on a message that had them — shows the line the caret
is on, rather than stopping dead at the first newline.

Also new: which-key opens by itself when a prefix has been left hanging past
`timeoutlen`, and lists only the keys that continue what you have already
pressed instead of all forty-five bindings.

Fixed, from v0.9.0 and earlier:

- Every conversation split charged two columns for a divider one column wide,
  leaving a dead strip down the right-hand edge — three columns wide with four
  conversations open.

- `:send` left the caption sitting in the composer if the upload succeeded but
  the local cache write failed. It looked unsent, and the next Enter posted it
  a second time as a message of its own.

- The terminal's own cursor stayed visible over the message list when an
  overlay covered the composer, so an input method drew its half-typed word on
  top of the conversation.

Every new key encoding here was measured through a real pty under both the
legacy and the kitty keyboard protocols rather than assumed — that check
exists because `<C-w>|` once passed its tests and bound nothing on a real
terminal. The caret itself went through an adversarial review that raised ten
findings; the seven real ones are fixed in this release, each verified by
reverting the fix and watching its own test fail.

macOS and Windows binaries are cross-compiled and verified as far as their
file format and the native renderer inside them. They have not been run.
